# Dress codes

Fitcheck tags every item `casual`, `smart-casual` or `formal`. Map the brief to those tags, then
pick within them.

## The ladder

| Code              | Reads as             | Build it from                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Black tie         | formal               | The most formal thing in the wardrobe: dinner jacket or a long/midi occasion dress, dress shoes. If nothing qualifies, say so rather than approximating. |
| Cocktail / formal | formal               | Tailoring, a midi or knee-length dress, leather shoes. A tie is optional in 2026; a jacket is not.                                                       |
| Business          | formal, smart-casual | Suit or blazer with tailored trousers, shirt, leather shoes. Quiet colours.                                                                              |
| Business casual   | smart-casual         | Chinos or tailored trousers, shirt or fine knit, loafers or clean leather trainers. No jacket needed.                                                    |
| Smart casual      | smart-casual         | Dark denim or chinos, knit or shirt, boots or clean leather trainers. One structured piece lifts the whole thing.                                        |
| Casual            | casual               | Jeans, tee or sweatshirt, trainers.                                                                                                                      |

When two tags are plausible, dress one notch up. Being slightly overdressed reads as effort;
underdressed reads as indifference.

## Common occasions

- **Wedding, day.** Smart-casual to formal, and a jacket unless it is a beach or garden wedding.
  Avoid white, cream and ivory entirely. Avoid all-black unless the couple asked for it. Standing on
  grass means block heels or flats, not stilettos.
- **Funeral.** Formal, dark, quiet. Navy and charcoal are fine where black is not available.
  No visible logos, no bright accents.
- **Interview.** One notch above what the company wears daily. Quiet colours, nothing new or stiff
  enough to fidget with. Tech and creative: smart casual with a structured jacket.
- **First date.** Smart casual you can move in. Something with a texture worth commenting on beats
  something formal. Comfortable shoes if there is any walking.
- **Smart restaurant.** Smart casual minimum, collar or a good knit, leather shoes. Many still turn
  away trainers and shorts.
- **Theatre, gallery, concert.** Smart casual. Layers, because the temperature is unpredictable.

## Practical constraints that beat the code

Ask or check before finalising:

- Outdoors for any length of time — add a real layer and drop the delicate shoes.
- Standing all evening — the shoes decide the outfit.
- Grass, gravel or cobbles — no thin heels.
- A venue with a coat check or none — an overcoat you must carry all night is a bad choice.

## What to say

Name the code you are dressing for and the one piece that anchors it. "Cocktail, so the navy blazer
does the work and the rest stays quiet." Then stop.
