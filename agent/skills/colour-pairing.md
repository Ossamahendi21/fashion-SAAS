# Colour pairing

## The shape of a good outfit

Two neutrals plus one accent is the default that almost always works. Three or more competing
saturated colours is the most common way an outfit goes wrong.

Neutrals: navy, charcoal, grey, black, white, cream, stone, camel, olive, denim blue, brown.
Treat denim and olive as neutrals — they behave like one even though they read as colours.

## Pairings that hold up

| Base            | Reliable partners                          | Accent                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Navy            | grey, white, cream, camel, brown, burgundy | rust, mustard, pale pink   |
| Charcoal / grey | white, black, navy, denim                  | emerald, burgundy, cobalt  |
| Camel / tan     | cream, white, navy, chocolate, olive       | forest green, burnt orange |
| Olive           | cream, stone, white, black, tan            | rust, mustard, burgundy    |
| Black           | white, grey, camel, denim                  | red, emerald, silver       |
| White / cream   | anything                                   | anything                   |

Navy with black is fine in 2026 — keep the textures different so it reads deliberate.
Brown with black is also fine; give the brown some warmth so it does not look like faded black.

## Three ways to combine

- **Tonal.** One colour, several depths — stone, oatmeal, camel. Quiet and expensive-looking. Vary
  the texture (knit against cotton against suede) or it flattens.
- **Neutral plus accent.** Neutral base, one saturated piece carrying the outfit. The accent is
  usually the smallest item: a scarf, a bag, shoes.
- **Complementary.** Opposite sides of the wheel — blue/orange, green/red, purple/yellow. Use the
  muted versions (navy and rust, olive and burgundy) rather than the primaries.

## Patterns

One pattern per outfit unless you are confident. Two work when the scales differ clearly (fine
pinstripe with a bold check) and they share a colour. Stripes and small checks behave as neutrals
next to a plain piece.

## Undertones

Warm (cream, camel, rust, olive, gold) and cool (white, grey, navy, burgundy, silver) each sit
comfortably with their own family. Crossing is fine; crossing in the two largest pieces is where it
starts to look accidental — keep one of them neutral.

## Layering

Inner to outer: the layer nearest the skin is the lightest in weight, not necessarily in colour.
Put the darkest piece at the bottom or the outside, so the eye settles. A light jacket over a dark
base is a deliberate look, not a default — call it out when you use it.

## Saying why

One sentence is enough: name the base, name the accent, say what the third element does.
"Navy chinos and the cream knit keep it quiet, and the tan boots warm it up" is the whole job.
